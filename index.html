Little Women

Written by: Louisa May Alcott

Read by: Elizabeth Klett

Louisa May Alcott's beloved 1868 novel is about the four March girls - Meg, Jo, Beth, and Amy - who are growing up in Massachusetts during the Civil War. As the novel opens, their father is away at war, and the girls are struggling to be good and to reconcile themselves to their relative poverty. Each has her trials to deal with, and they are encouraged by their loving mother, and by their friendship with their neighbor, Theodore "Laurie" Laurence.

Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde

Written by: Robert Louis Stevenson

Read by: Phil Chenevert

This story deals with the obvious fact that we humans are split, dual. We have urges to do the 'right' thing, to be honorable and wise, but we also frequently fail to follow these better instincts and follow instead urges to do dishonorable, evil things. We seem to battle within ourselves. Are we really composed of two different personalities housed within the same brain, within the same person? Dr Jekyll in this story is so convinced and manages by scientific means to actually split himself into his ordinary composite self, and his evil self whom he calls Mr. Hyde. The horror of this unnatural split is well documented here and shows what might happen were this possible. (summary by phil chenevert)

I Have No Mouth And I Must Scream

Written by: Harlan Ellison

I Have No Mouth, and I Must Scream is a bleak 1967 sci-fi horror story by Harlan Ellison. A sentient supercomputer, AM, annihilates humanity but keeps five people alive, tormenting them for eternity in a subterranean hell. The plot follows these survivors' desperate, hopeless journey for food, exploring themes of existential despair and humanity's powerlessness against a cruel, omnipotent AI.
